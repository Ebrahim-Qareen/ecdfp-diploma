S6 — Network Forensics, Timelines, Reporting & Capstone

Wireshark and tcpdump, network file carving, plaso super-timelines, and the full written capstone report.

This is the syllabus, not the session. The teaching pages publish one session at a time in Phase 4. When S6 ships, its card on the home page links to the teaching page and this brief stays here as the syllabus.

What you will be able to do

By the end of the session, unaided, at your own keyboard.

  • Filter a capture down to the traffic that matters and follow a stream to a conclusion.
  • Recognise beaconing by interval and by DNS behaviour, not by reputation alone.
  • Carve a transferred file out of a capture and hash it against the on-disk copy.
  • Build a super-timeline from $MFT, event logs and network evidence, then pivot inside it.
  • Assemble six sessions of findings into one report and grade it against the rubric yourself.

Opens from

S5 — the host story is complete. S6 adds the wire, then the timeline that joins the two.

Closes with

The capstone investigation, run in class. The written report is take-home.

What you hand in

One report, graded on the same four criteria as every other session. The rubric never changes — that is what makes your progress measurable. The report and its rubric →

CriterionWhat is graded
1 · IntegrityHashes recorded before and after, chain of custody complete, working copy demonstrated.
2 · MethodWhat you did, with which tool and version, in an order another analyst could repeat.
3 · FindingsFact only. Every finding names its artifact and gives the exact path. No verbs of inference.
4 · SeparationInterpretation kept visibly distinct from findings, and the limitations stated outright.

Deliverable — Chain-of-Custody Record

One unbroken custody record runs across the whole course. This session's record carries every earlier session forward and adds its own — the authorization gate, this session's custody steps, and the running transfer log. Fill it as you work; the exported PDF/HTML is the record you submit. Structure follows SWGDE 18-Q-002, SWGDE 18-F-002 and ISO/IEC 27037. Open the S6 Chain-of-Custody record →

It is one record, not six. The custody-transfer log and the disposition of originals are case-level — clearing a session never erases them. You do not silently erase a chain.
