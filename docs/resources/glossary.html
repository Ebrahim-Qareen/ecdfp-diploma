Every term · what it proves · what it does not

The glossary

A forensic term is only useful with its limit attached. Every entry here carries two lines — what the thing proves, and what it does not — in the same colours the course uses for findings and limitations. Read the second line as carefully as the first; it is where reports go wrong.

Method & the report P01 · P14

Finding

One observable fact, past tense, tied to one named artifact at an exact path with a UTC time. Report §8.

provesThat the artifact recorded exactly this, and another examiner can reproduce it from the bytes.
does not proveWhy it is there, who caused it, or what it means. If the sentence needs because, it is not a finding.

Interpretation

What findings mean, citing them, with a stated confidence and an alternative you could not exclude. Report §9.

provesNothing on its own — it is your reasoning, made visible and checkable.
does not proveAnything a cited finding does not support. Without a bracketed finding under it, it is opinion.

Limitation · “cannot prove”

What could not be determined, why, and what that does to the conclusions. Report §10, graded.

provesThat you looked for the edge of your evidence and found it.
does not prove— a report with no limitation reads as one that did not look.

Confidence

How far you would defend an interpretation: high / medium / low, in words, never “certain”.

provesHow many independent artifacts agree.
does not proveTruth. High confidence with one artifact is a contradiction.

Chain of custody

One line per transfer of an exhibit: who released, who received, when, from where, why, two signatures. Report §4.

provesThat the exhibit was accounted for at every moment between seizure and analysis.
does not proveAnything during a gap. A gap declared is a weakness; a gap found by the other side is the end of the exhibit.

Exhibit

A physical or logical thing seized or produced, with an ID, a description, and both hashes. Report §5.

provesWhat you examined, and that it is what was seized.
does not proveThat it is the right thing to have seized — a perfectly verified image of the wrong disk is still the wrong disk.

Rubric · the four criteria

Integrity (§4–5) · Method (§6–7) · Findings (§8) · Separation (§9–10). Unchanged for the whole course.

provesWhat the report is graded on — and criterion 4 is the skill the diploma exists to teach.
does not prove— the rubric never rewards a correct conclusion reached by an unsupported step.

One adjective past the evidence

The course’s one writing rule: state the claim in the verb the evidence supports, cite the artifact, give confidence, name the limit.

proves— it is the test every sentence of §8 is read against.
does not prove— “a suspicious archive” is a finding with one word of interpretation smuggled in.

Integrity & acquisition P02 · P05

Hash · SHA-256 / MD5

A fixed-length fingerprint of bytes; one bit changed changes the whole value (the avalanche). SHA-256 is the value you defend; MD5 is a lookup key only.

provesThat two byte sequences are identical (match) or not (mismatch).
does not proveWho made the bytes, when, whether they are complete, or whether they are the right bytes. A hash matches a file, not a story.

Verified

A tool’s word for a write-then-read round trip inside itself: what it wrote equals what it read back.

provesThe image file survived the tool’s own copy.
does not proveThat the source was read correctly, that the blocker worked, that the right device was selected, or that the disk was complete. Verified ≠ complete ≠ correct.

Independent hash of the source

Hashing the original device with a second tool (dc3dd, sha256sum) and comparing to the image’s hash.

provesThe image equals the device — the only statement that is about the device rather than your file.
does not proveCompleteness beyond the capacity the device reported (see HPA / DCO). Practical on 16 GB; hours on 4 TB — know what you give up.

Write blocker

Hardware (Tableau) or software that lets a device be read but not written during acquisition.

provesNothing by itself — its log proves the device was not written during imaging.
does not proveAnything if it is not logged and named in §7. “Behind a blocker” without a log is a claim, not a control.

Physical · logical · targeted

Physical = every sector; logical = one volume; targeted = a chosen set of files (a collection, not an image).

provesWhat scope the image can speak to.
does not proveAnything outside that scope. A logical image never sees a hidden partition, and the tool will not tell you.

raw · E01 · AD1

raw (dd): bytes only, needs a sidecar. E01: per-block CRC, embedded hash and case metadata. AD1: a logical collection, not a disk image (Autopsy cannot open it).

provesE01’s metadata proves what the examiner typed at acquisition.
does not proveThat the typed fields are true, or that the disk was complete or correct. The container stores claims; it does not check them.

HPA · DCO

Host Protected Area and Device Configuration Overlay: capacity a disk can hide from the OS. One disk can report three sizes.

proves(when checked) what the true native capacity is — hdparm --dco-identify first, then hdparm -N.
does not proveAnything in the wrong order: a DCO lowers what -N measures against, so HPA-first reports “disabled” on a disk that is hiding capacity.

Snapshot

A VMware restore point of the analyst workstation.

provesNothing about evidence. It protects your machine.
does not proveIntegrity of anything; it never appears in a chain of custody. Evidence is protected by hashes and by working on a copy.

Bytes, files & what hides in them P03 · P06 · P07

Magic bytes · file signature

The first bytes of a file that identify its format: 25 50 44 46 PDF, 89 50 4E 47 PNG, FF D8 FF JPEG, 50 4B 03 04 ZIP (and docx, jar, apk).

provesWhat the file starts as, regardless of its extension.
does not proveWhat follows the header. A real JPEG can carry a ZIP after FF D9 and still pass the signature check.

Metadata · EXIF

Data about a file written by software: DateTimeOriginal, ModifyDate, Software, GPS, an embedded thumbnail. All editable.

provesWhat some software wrote into the file at some point.
does not proveThat the values are true. The file records a timestamp; it does not witness it. Three dates on one photo is normal.

Appended data · polyglot

Bytes after a file’s end marker (appended), or a file valid as two formats at once (polyglot).

provesThat something was placed there — found only by reading past the end marker.
does not proveWho placed it or why. Detection proves presence, never intent.

LSB steganography · channel asymmetry

Data hidden in the least-significant bits of pixels. Natural noise spreads across R, G and B; a payload does not.

proves(asymmetry across channels) that the pixel data is not natural noise — usable without a clean twin, which is always.
does not proveContent until extracted, or purpose ever.

Redaction

Removing content from an image. Drawing a box over pixels updates the main image and leaves the EXIF thumbnail untouched.

proves(a thumbnail that differs from its image) that the file was edited after the thumbnail was made.
does not prove— real redaction means exporting a new file, not painting the old one.

Macro · vbaProject.bin

Code stored inside an Office document; visible as a member of the document’s ZIP without opening it in Office.

provesThat code is present in the document.
does not proveThat it ran, that macros were enabled, that anyone clicked Enable Content, or that the code is hostile. And the reverse fails too: the second delivery path needs no macro.

Entropy

A measure of randomness per section, 0–8. Zeros 0.00, text ~5.4, code ~6, compressed 7.96, random 7.95.

provesThat a region is (or is not) worth a closer look.
does not proveMalice, or even packing vs. compression — 7.96 and 7.95 are indistinguishable. A reason to look, never a finding.

Import table

The functions a PE binary declares it will use from system libraries, read statically.

provesWhat the binary can do — a capability list.
does not proveWhat it did, or what it resolves at run time. A very short import table is a louder signal than a long one: the capability is hidden until it runs.

Memory & volatile evidence P04

Order of volatility

Collect what disappears fastest first: registers/cache → memory → network state → running processes → disk → backups (RFC 3227).

proves— it is a decision rule, not evidence: collect first, examine second.
does not prove— a hash taken after power-down compares itself to nothing.

Memory image

A capture of RAM (WinPmem writes raw), read with Volatility 3 — no profiles, no imageinfo.

provesWhat was resident in memory across the seconds the capture took.
does not proveA consistent instant. The capture is a smear: an inconsistent process list is not anti-forensics, it is time passing.

Smear

The inconsistency in a memory image because the machine kept running while it was captured.

provesThat the machine was live during capture.
does not proveTampering. Treat contradictions as a lead to verify, not a finding.

windows.cmdline · PEB

A process’s command line as read from its own Process Environment Block — memory the process can rewrite.

provesWhat the PEB contained at capture.
does not proveWhat was actually executed. A command line is a lead, not proof; corroborate with prefetch, logs, or the binary itself.

Disk & file systems P08 · P09

Sector · cluster · slack

Disks are addressed in 512-byte sectors; file systems allocate in clusters of several sectors; the unused tail of a file’s last cluster is slack.

proves(slack) what was on the disk before this file was written there — one byte past a boundary costs a whole cluster.
does not proveThat the current file’s owner ever saw it. Slack is the previous occupant’s leftovers.

MBR · GPT

Partition tables. MBR: 64 bytes at 0x1BE and the 55 AA signature. GPT: EFI PART at LBA 1, CRC32s, a backup header at the last LBA.

provesWhere the OS was told the partitions are.
does not proveWhere data is. Zeroing 512 bytes makes a disk unmountable and leaves 99.9992% of it untouched.

Partition-table recovery · BPB_BkBootSec

Rebuilding a wiped table from boot sectors found by scanning. A FAT32 volume keeps a backup boot sector at the offset its own BPB records (usually 6).

proves(the discarded candidate) why a third boot sector is a backup, not a partition.
does not proveCorrectness by mounting: a table built from all three candidates mounts too. The wrong answer that works is the lesson.

FAT delete · 0xE5

Deleting on FAT overwrites the first byte of the directory entry with E5 and frees the cluster chain. Five bytes change; the data does not.

provesThat the file was deleted, and (from the surviving entry) its size and first cluster.
does not proveThat the content is gone — the first cluster still begins %PDF until something overwrites it.

MFT record · resident / non-resident

Every NTFS file has a 1,024-byte record in $MFT. Small files keep their content inside the record (resident); large ones keep a run list pointing to clusters.

proves(a deleted resident file) that its entire content is still readable in the record deletion marked free.
does not proveWhen it was last read, or by whom. NTFS records what the file is, not who touched it.

Alternate data stream (ADS)

A second named $DATA stream on an NTFS file. Directory listings show only the unnamed stream’s size.

provesThat data exists on the file beyond what the listing shows.
does not proveIntent — Windows itself writes ADS (Zone.Identifier).

Carving

Recovering files by header and footer patterns alone, without consulting the file system.

provesThat the bytes of a file exist on the media — “deleted” means nothing to a carver.
does not proveBoundaries: a footer is a guess (a PDF ending %%EOF\n carves one byte short), and a fragmented file carves with someone else’s clusters inside it.

Windows artifacts — one verb each P10 · P11

Registry hive

Files (SYSTEM, SOFTWARE, SAM, SECURITY, NTUSER.DAT, UsrClass.dat) behind the one tree regedit shows. A regf header with two sequence numbers.

proves(sequence numbers one apart) that the hive was not cleanly flushed — an abrupt power-off.
does not proveThat every hive came from the same machine. Check shared volume GUIDs before correlating.

USBSTOR · MountedDevices · MountPoints2

The USB chain: device serial (SYSTEM) → drive letter and volume GUID (MountedDevices) → the user profile that mounted it (NTUSER.DAT).

provesThat a device with that serial was connected, which letter it took, and under which profile.
does not proveWhich person was at the keyboard, or that any file was written to it. MountPoints2 names an account; nothing here names a human.

First InstallDate

The date Windows installed (or reinstalled) a device’s driver instance.

provesWhen a driver instance was created.
does not proveFirst connection. It is often the latest of four dates; the earliest sits inside the volume GUID’s UUIDv1 clock, which nobody decodes by default.

Prefetch

.pf files Windows keeps to speed up program starts: run count, last eight run times, files touched.

provesThe program ran, how many times, when.
does not proveWho ran it, or why.

ShimCache · Amcache

Application-compatibility records of executables Windows has seen on disk.

provesThe file was present on the disk.
does not proveThat it ran. The ShimCache timestamp is the file’s modification time, not a run time. Upgrading “present” to “ran” is the error P11 exists to prevent.

UserAssist

A per-user count and focus time for programs launched through the Windows GUI.

provesThe profile launched it from the interface, and for how long it had focus.
does not proveLaunches from scripts, services or the command line, which it never records.

ShellBags

Explorer’s memory of folder windows a user opened, with paths and view settings.

provesA folder was browsed under that profile — even on a drive that is no longer present.
does not proveThat any file in it was opened, or that a drive letter is the same device as another artifact’s letter. A letter is not a device.

LNK · jump list

Shortcut files and per-application recent lists Windows writes when files are opened.

provesA file at that path was opened, with the target’s size and timestamps at the time.
does not proveThat the file was copied anywhere, or by whom. An E:\ path proves access at that letter, not a transfer to it.

Recycle Bin · $I / $R

On deletion, $R holds the content and $I the original path, size and deletion time.

provesA file was binned, from where, when, and how large it was.
does not proveWho deleted it, or that two deletions eight seconds apart were one act — that is interpretation.

Event logs P12

Event log

The one artifact Windows writes on purpose: EventID + Provider + UTC time + the fields that carry the meaning.

provesWhat the logging service was told, when.
does not proveWhat it was never told: persistence via a Run key is invisible to the Security log (Sysmon 13 sees it).

1102 · 104 — log cleared

Security (1102) or another log (104) was cleared, by which account, when. MITRE T1070.001.

provesThat someone with that account tried to silence the testimony — the loudest testimony of all.
does not proveWhat was in the cleared window. Rebuild it from artifacts that live off the logs: prefetch, registry, $MFT, pcap.

4624 · LogonType

A successful logon; type 2 interactive, 3 network, 9 NewCredentials (runas /netonly), 10 RDP.

provesAn account authenticated, and how.
does not proveA human. “Interactive” describes the session type — a Chrome updater can produce type 2.

Pass-the-hash pattern

4624 type 9 + package seclogo + source ::1 + 4672 special privileges, together.

proves(the combination) a credential used without its password — MITRE T1550.002.
does not proveAnything from any one of those events alone.

4732 · group membership

An account was added to a security-enabled local group, e.g. Guest into Administrators. MITRE T1098.

provesThe change, the subject account, the time.
does not proveWho sat at the keyboard, or whether the new member was ever used.

Network P13

Beacon

A host contacting a server at a fixed interval — ten GETs every 30 seconds with zero jitter is not a person browsing.

proves(the regularity) automated command-and-control traffic — and it survives encryption, because timing is metadata.
does not proveWhat was said. Content needs the payload; the rhythm does not.

DNS tunnel

Data carried in DNS queries — long Base32 TXT lookups to a strange domain where an A record under 30 bytes belongs.

proves(the shape) that DNS is carrying something DNS does not carry. Decode afterwards to confirm content.
does not proveThe destination of the data beyond the resolver it reached.

C2 · exfiltration

Command-and-control: the channel the malware takes orders on. Exfiltration: data leaving — here by DNS and by a plain POST to a raw IP.

proves(one IP across the pcap and the email header) that delivery, command channel and exfil are one operation — the strongest fact in the case.
does not proveThe person behind the IP. IP ≠ person, always.

Email header · SPF / DKIM / DMARC

The Received: chain and three authentication checks on a message’s claimed sender.

provesWhich server handed the mail on, and whether the sending domain vouched for it.
does not proveForgery from a single failure — misconfiguration exists. Three failures plus a raw-IP Reply-To is a strong interpretation, still an interpretation.

User-Agent

The client string in an HTTP request — MSIE 6.0 among modern Chrome traffic.

provesWhat the client claimed to be.
does not proveWhat it was. A stale UA is an anomaly worth following, not evidence on its own.

Timelines P14

Super-timeline

Every artifact type (registry, $MFT, prefetch, evtx, browser, pcap) on one UTC axis — plaso’s log2timeline then psort. A million events you never read top to bottom.

provesOrder across sources, which no single source shows — and causation lives in the order.
does not proveAnything you did not pivot to. The skill is choosing the window, not reading the file.

Normalise · pivot

Convert every timestamp to one clock (UTC, timezone-corrected), then filter to the window or indicator that matters.

proves(normalising) that events from different sources can be compared at all.
does not prove— skip the timezone and every event is wrong by the offset, consistently, which is the worst kind of wrong.

Timestomp · $SI vs $FN

Forging a file’s timestamps. Tools rewrite $STANDARD_INFORMATION; the kernel-maintained $FILE_NAME attribute is left behind.

proves(SI and FN disagreeing) that a timestamp was altered.
does not proveBy whom — but faking one artifact does not fake the others, which is the whole basis of cross-validation.

The window the attacker erased

Logs cleared at the end of an intrusion remove the logs — and nothing else.

proves(a timeline rebuilt from prefetch, registry, $MFT and pcap) the sequence the clearing was meant to hide.
does not proveEvents that lived only in the logs. State that line in §10.

Using this page. When you write a finding, look the artifact up here and read its second line before you choose a verb. When a classmate’s report says an artifact proves something, check it against the same line. Every entry maps to the page that taught it — the page number is beside each heading.
