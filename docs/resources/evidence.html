Publishing policy

This site publishes manifests, hashes and acquisition logs — never evidence bytes. Images are distributed offline. Every set states its sourcing tier, its provenance and its licence, and public corpora are linked, never rehosted.

Before every session

Evidence for session N is published by the end of session N−1, always with both hashes.

  1. Download the set for the coming session.
  2. Compute MD5 and SHA-256 and compare both against the published manifest.
  3. Record the result in your chain-of-custody form before you open anything.
If a hash does not match

That is a finding. Bring it to the session and report it — do not re-download quietly and do not work on it. The instructor keeps a fallback USB set for exactly this case.

What a matching hash proves

That the bytes you hold are the bytes that were hashed. It does not prove where they came from, who made them, or that they were acquired correctly — that is what the chain of custody and the acquisition log are for.

Sourcing tiers

TierMeaningRule
Tier 1Acquired in our own lab from machines we builtFull provenance. Published as manifest + hashes + acquisition log.
Tier 2Public forensic corpora with a clear licenceLinked, never rehosted. Students download from the source and verify against its published hash.
Tier 3Synthesized for a specific teaching pointGeneration method recorded so the set can be rebuilt identically.

Disk images, memory dumps, event logs and registry hives are Tier 1 or Tier 2 only. Nothing that a student is asked to treat as evidence is invented.

Chain of custody

Six fields, every transfer

Who handled it · what it is · when · from where · hash at the moment of transfer · where stored afterwards.

The same form is used in all six sessions and the last 15 minutes of every session are spent completing it. It is not paperwork practice — an unbroken chain is what makes the rest of your work usable.
