The report and its rubric

The report is the assessment of record. There is no exam score kept on this site and no quiz result stored anywhere — you are measured on what you can write down and defend.

The rubric

Identical in all six sessions. You see the same target six times, which is the only way to make progress in report writing visible — to you and to the instructor.

#CriterionWhat a pass looks like
1IntegrityHashes recorded before and after handling, both algorithms. Chain of custody complete. You worked on a copy and can show it.
2MethodWhat you did, with which tool and version, in an order another analyst could repeat and get your result.
3FindingsFact only. Every finding names its artifact and gives the exact path. No verbs of inference — nothing "suggests", "indicates" or "was likely" in this section.
4SeparationInterpretation kept visibly distinct from findings, each interpretation referencing at least one finding, and the limitations stated outright rather than left implied.

Criterion 4, worked

The separation between fact and inference is the skill this course exists to teach. Three boxes, one artifact:

Finding

SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Enum\USBSTOR contains a subkey for a device with serial 07A1…, first install timestamp 2026-03-14 09:12:41Z. Parsed with RECmd 2.x from the hive at C:\Windows\System32\config\SYSTEM inside the mounted image.

Interpretation

A removable device with that serial was attached to this host on or before that timestamp, while this hive was live.

Cannot prove

Who attached it, that any file was copied to it, or that it was attached again afterwards. Those need setupapi.dev.log, LNK targets and shellbags — and even together they may not close it.

Note what makes the first box a finding: an artifact, an exact path, a tool with a version, and not one word of inference. The moment “which suggests the user copied files” appears in that box, criterion 4 fails — however correct the conclusion turns out to be.

The template

SectionContains
1 · SummaryWhat you were asked, what you did, what you concluded — in that order, on one page.
2 · Evidence handledEach item: what it is, where it came from, both hashes, when you received it.
3 · MethodTools with versions, and the sequence. Enough for someone else to repeat it.
4 · FindingsOne per artifact. Fact, path, timestamp. Nothing else.
5 · InterpretationWhat the findings taken together support — each pointing back to its findings.
6 · LimitationsWhat this evidence cannot establish, and what would be needed instead.
7 · Chain of custodyThe form, complete.
Secrets and personal data

Passwords, hashes, keys and a data subject's personal information never appear in the body of a report. They enter by reference only — path, offset, timestamp, record count — and the values themselves go in a restricted appendix that is handled separately. An authenticator is not evidence; publishing one in a report is a finding against you.
