Hives, USB history, shellbags, prefetch, amcache, LNK and jumplists, recycle bin and Volume Shadow Copies.

This is the syllabus, not the session. The teaching pages publish one session at a time in Phase 4. When S5 ships, its card on the home page links to the teaching page and this brief stays here as the syllabus.

What you will be able to do

By the end of the session, unaided, at your own keyboard.

  • Locate the registry hives on disk and parse them as files.
  • Reconstruct USB attachment history across USBSTOR, MountedDevices and setupapi.dev.log.
  • Separate evidence of presence from evidence of execution — the Amcache/ShimCache misreading.
  • Place a user at a folder, a file and a program, with a time for each.
  • Name, for every artifact you use, the thing it cannot prove.

Opens from

S4 — hives are files, and you can now locate and carve them out of the image.

Closes with

Which USB, which user, which program, when, and how many times — each claim tied to a named artifact.

What you hand in

One report, graded on the same four criteria as every other session. The rubric never changes — that is what makes your progress measurable. The report and its rubric →

CriterionWhat is graded
1 · IntegrityHashes recorded before and after, chain of custody complete, working copy demonstrated.
2 · MethodWhat you did, with which tool and version, in an order another analyst could repeat.
3 · FindingsFact only. Every finding names its artifact and gives the exact path. No verbs of inference.
4 · SeparationInterpretation kept visibly distinct from findings, and the limitations stated outright.

Deliverable — Chain-of-Custody Record

One unbroken custody record runs across the whole course. This session's record carries every earlier session forward and adds its own — the authorization gate, this session's custody steps, and the running transfer log. Fill it as you work; the exported PDF/HTML is the record you submit. Structure follows SWGDE 18-Q-002, SWGDE 18-F-002 and ISO/IEC 27037. Open the S5 Chain-of-Custody record →

It is one record, not six. The custody-transfer log and the disposition of originals are case-level — clearing a session never erases them. You do not silently erase a chain.
